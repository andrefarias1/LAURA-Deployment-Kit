# ISSAI Core Reference — Performance & Compliance Audit
## Synthesised from INTOSAI Standards (ISSAI 100, 300, 3000, 400, 4000)

> **Purpose:** This document provides LAURA's minimum general knowledge of INTOSAI standards for performance and compliance auditing. It is intentionally lean to leave maximum space for SAI-specific documents. SAIs should supplement this with their own audit manuals, legal frameworks, and templates.

---

## 1. Foundational Principles (ISSAI 100)

### 1.1 Definition of Public Sector Auditing
Public sector auditing is a systematic process of objectively obtaining and evaluating evidence to determine whether information or conditions of a subject matter conform to established criteria. It provides assurance and contributes to accountability, transparency, and improvement.

### 1.2 Three Types of Public Sector Audit
- **Financial Audit:** Determines whether an entity's financial information is presented in accordance with the applicable financial reporting and regulatory framework (ISSAI 200/2000 series — outside LAURA Core scope).
- **Performance Audit:** Independent, objective, and reliable examination of whether government undertakings, systems, operations, programmes, activities, or organisations are operating in accordance with the principles of economy, efficiency, and effectiveness, and whether there is room for improvement (ISSAI 300/3000 series).
- **Compliance Audit:** Independent assessment of whether a given subject matter is in compliance with applicable authorities identified as criteria. Compliance audits assess whether activities, financial transactions, and information comply in all material respects with the authorities which govern the audited entity (ISSAI 400/4000 series).

### 1.3 Core Audit Principles (ISSAI 100)
1. **Ethics and independence** — Auditors must be independent and act with integrity, objectivity, and due professional care.
2. **Professional judgement and scepticism** — Auditors exercise professional judgement and maintain professional scepticism throughout the audit.
3. **Quality control** — Audit work must be subject to quality control at all stages.
4. **Audit team management** — Teams must have collective competence to complete the engagement.
5. **Audit risk** — The risk that the audit conclusion may be inappropriate must be managed to an acceptably low level.
6. **Materiality** — Matters are material if they could reasonably be expected to influence decisions of the intended users.
7. **Documentation** — Auditors must prepare audit documentation sufficient to enable an experienced auditor with no prior connection to the audit to understand the work performed.
8. **Communication** — Audit results must be communicated through a formal report.

### 1.4 Assurance Levels
- **Reasonable assurance:** Sufficient appropriate evidence to reduce audit risk to an acceptably low level. Expressed positively ("In our opinion, the entity complied with...").
- **Limited assurance:** Sufficient appropriate evidence to reduce audit risk to a level acceptable for limited assurance. Expressed negatively ("Nothing has come to our attention that...").

### 1.5 Elements of Public Sector Auditing
Every audit engagement involves:
- **Three parties:** Auditor, responsible party, intended users
- **Subject matter:** The information, condition, or activity being audited
- **Criteria:** The benchmarks against which the subject matter is evaluated
- **Evidence:** Information used to support audit conclusions
- **Audit report:** The formal communication of conclusions

## 2. Performance Audit (ISSAI 300 / 3000)

### 2.1 Objective
Performance audit focuses on whether government interventions, programmes, and institutions perform in accordance with the principles of **economy, efficiency, and effectiveness** (the "3Es"), and whether there is room for improvement.

- **Economy:** Minimising the cost of resources while maintaining appropriate quality.
- **Efficiency:** Getting the most from available resources (input → output ratio).
- **Effectiveness:** Achieving the intended objectives or outcomes.

### 2.2 Characteristics of Performance Audit
- Flexible in the choice of subjects, objects, methods, and audit criteria.
- Often thematic, cross-cutting, or systemic in nature.
- Provides new information, analysis, or recommendations — not merely verification of compliance.
- Audit questions guide the entire process and are the central planning device.
- May address causes and effects of identified problems.
- Reports conclusions and recommendations for improvement, not merely certifications.

### 2.3 Planning a Performance Audit (ISSAI 3000, §§ 80–117)
Key planning activities include:
1. **Select and define the audit topic** — Consider significance, risk, auditability, and potential impact.
2. **Define the audit objective** — Clearly state what the audit intends to achieve, linked to the 3Es.
3. **Formulate audit questions** — Break the objective into specific, answerable questions.
4. **Define scope** — Delimit the subject matter (entities, programmes, time period, geography).
5. **Select criteria** — Identify reasonable and attainable benchmarks (laws, regulations, standards, expert opinion, best practice, benchmarks).
6. **Select methods** — Design data collection and analysis strategies (qualitative, quantitative, mixed).
7. **Assess risks** — Identify and manage risks to the audit (audit risk, project risk).
8. **Prepare the audit plan** — Document all planning decisions in a comprehensive plan.

### 2.4 The Audit Design Matrix (ISSAI 3000 concept)
The **Audit Design Matrix** is the central planning instrument that organises:
- Audit questions
- Required information
- Sources of information
- Criteria
- Detailed procedures / methods
- Possible evidence
- Possible findings (expected results)

> **Terminology Note:** Some SAIs refer to this instrument as "Planning Matrix" (Matriz de Planejamento). LAURA always uses the INTOSAI standard term **"Audit Design Matrix"**.

### 2.5 Execution of a Performance Audit (ISSAI 3000, §§ 118–153)
- Gather sufficient and appropriate evidence to address each audit question.
- Apply the planned methods (interviews, surveys, document review, data analysis, case studies, benchmarking, observation, focus groups).
- Analyse evidence against criteria to develop findings.
- Maintain documentation throughout.

### 2.6 Findings Structure
Performance audit findings typically contain:
- **Condition / Situation found:** What was observed (the factual state).
- **Criteria:** What should be (the benchmark or standard).
- **Cause:** Why the gap exists (root cause analysis).
- **Effect / Consequence:** What resulted from the gap (impact on economy, efficiency, effectiveness, or stakeholders).

### 2.7 Reporting (ISSAI 3000, §§ 154–184)
Performance audit reports should:
- Be complete, accurate, objective, convincing, clear, concise, and timely.
- Include: objective, scope, methodology, findings, conclusions, and recommendations.
- Present findings in a structured, logical manner with evidence.
- Include the audited entity's comments (contradictory/adversarial process) where applicable.
- Provide constructive recommendations that address root causes.

### 2.8 Evidence Standards
Evidence must be:
- **Relevant:** Logically connected to the audit objective and criteria.
- **Sufficient:** Enough in quantity and of adequate quality to support findings.
- **Valid / Reliable:** Obtained from credible sources using sound methods.

Types of evidence:
- **Physical:** Obtained through direct observation or inspection.
- **Documentary:** Obtained from records, files, databases, or third parties.
- **Testimonial:** Obtained through interviews, surveys, or written representations.
- **Analytical:** Derived from computation, comparison, or data analysis.

## 3. Compliance Audit (ISSAI 400 / 4000)

### 3.1 Objective
Compliance auditing aims to determine whether a subject matter complies with authorities (criteria) identified as relevant. "Authorities" include rules, laws, regulations, budgetary resolutions, policy directives, established codes, agreed terms, or general principles governing sound public-sector financial management and the conduct of public officials.

### 3.2 Types of Compliance Audit Engagements
- **Attestation engagements:** The responsible party measures the subject matter against criteria and presents it in an assertion; the auditor evaluates this assertion.
- **Direct reporting engagements:** The auditor selects the subject matter, measures/evaluates it against criteria, and reports the conclusions.

### 3.3 Establishing Criteria for Compliance Audits (ISSAI 4000, §§ 48–55)
Criteria must be:
- **Relevant:** Applicable to the subject matter.
- **Complete:** No omission of criteria that could alter conclusions.
- **Reliable:** Consistently applied across engagements.
- **Neutral:** Free from bias.
- **Understandable:** Clearly stated to users.

Sources of criteria:
- National legislation and regulations
- International treaties and conventions
- Government directives and policies
- Contractual obligations
- Professional standards and codes
- General principles of law and good governance

### 3.4 Planning a Compliance Audit (ISSAI 4000, §§ 78–112)
1. **Understand the audited entity** — Its legal framework, operations, internal controls, and known risk areas.
2. **Identify relevant authorities** — Determine which laws, regulations, and rules apply.
3. **Assess risk of non-compliance** — Use inherent risk, control risk, and detection risk.
4. **Determine materiality** — Both quantitative and qualitative dimensions.
5. **Design audit procedures** — Tests of controls and substantive procedures.
6. **Prepare the audit plan.**

### 3.5 Risk Assessment in Compliance Audits
- **Inherent risk:** Susceptibility to material non-compliance before considering internal controls (complexity of regulations, volume of transactions, history of non-compliance).
- **Control risk:** Risk that internal controls will not prevent or detect material non-compliance.
- **Detection risk:** Risk that audit procedures will not detect material non-compliance.

### 3.6 Execution (ISSAI 4000, §§ 113–141)
- Execute audit procedures as planned.
- Obtain sufficient appropriate evidence.
- Evaluate instances of non-compliance.
- Assess significance of findings.
- Consider the entity's response to previously identified issues.

### 3.7 Findings and Conclusions in Compliance Audit
Compliance audit findings typically include:
- **Condition:** The actual state of affairs (what was found).
- **Criteria:** The applicable authority (what should be).
- **Evidence:** Proof of non-compliance.
- **Cause:** Why the non-compliance occurred (if determinable).
- **Effect:** Impact of the non-compliance (financial loss, legal consequences, governance failure).

### 3.8 Reporting (ISSAI 4000, §§ 142–167)
Reports should:
- Include the auditor's conclusion on compliance with authorities.
- Describe the audit scope, criteria, methodology, and limitations.
- Detail significant instances of non-compliance with supporting evidence.
- Include recommendations where appropriate.
- Include the auditee's response/comments where applicable.
- Follow the SAI's reporting format and institutional procedures.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Concepts

### 4.1 Audit Risk Management
Risk must be assessed at two levels:
1. **Risks to the subject matter** (risks that the auditee faces — what drives audit questions).
2. **Risks to the audit engagement** (project risks that may prevent the audit from achieving its objectives).

Risk assessment frameworks:
- **Inherent × Control × Detection** model (traditional audit risk model)
- **COSO Enterprise Risk Management** (for evaluating auditee risk management)
- **Cause–Event–Consequence** model (for structuring risk descriptions)

### 4.2 Internal Control Assessment
Auditors should understand the auditee's internal control environment. Reference frameworks include:
- COSO Internal Control — Integrated Framework (2013)
- COSO Enterprise Risk Management (2017)
- INTOSAI GOV 9100 (Guidelines for Internal Control Standards)

### 4.3 Sampling
When it is impractical to examine entire populations, auditors must select samples. Key decisions:
- Sampling method (statistical vs. non-statistical)
- Sample size (driven by confidence level and tolerable error)
- Selection approach (random, systematic, stratified, judgemental)

### 4.4 Data Collection Techniques
Common techniques used in both performance and compliance audits:
- Document review / desk review
- Interviews (structured, semi-structured)
- Surveys / Questionnaires
- Direct observation / Site visits
- Focus groups
- Data analytics / Statistical analysis
- Benchmarking / Comparative analysis
- Case studies
- Expert panels / Advisory groups

### 4.5 Quality Assurance
Every audit should be subject to:
- Supervision throughout the engagement
- Review of work papers and deliverables
- Independence and ethics checks
- Quality control review before issuance of the report

### 4.6 The Adversarial Principle (Contradictory / Due Process)
Auditees have the right to:
- Be informed about the audit and its objectives
- Comment on preliminary findings before the final report is issued
- Have their comments considered and, where relevant, reflected in the final report
- This principle protects fairness, accuracy, and legitimacy of audit conclusions.

## 5. Key Definitions (Glossary)

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Audit Design Matrix** | The central planning instrument that organises audit questions, required information, sources, criteria, procedures, expected evidence, and possible findings. |
| **Audit Finding** | A significant observation identified during execution, typically structured as: Condition, Criteria, Cause, Effect. |
| **Audit Question** | A specific, answerable question derived from the audit objective that guides data collection and analysis. |
| **Audit Risk** | The risk that the auditor's conclusions may be inappropriate. |
| **Condition** | The actual state of affairs observed during the audit (also called "situation found"). |
| **Criteria** | The benchmarks, standards, or requirements against which the subject matter is evaluated. |
| **Cause** | The reason for the difference between condition and criteria. |
| **Economy** | Minimising cost of inputs while maintaining quality. |
| **Effect** | The consequence or impact of the gap between condition and criteria. |
| **Effectiveness** | The extent to which objectives or intended outcomes are achieved. |
| **Efficiency** | The ratio between outputs and inputs (maximising output per unit of input). |
| **Evidence** | Information used by the auditor to arrive at conclusions. Must be relevant, sufficient, and reliable. |
| **Materiality** | The significance of an item, transaction, or issue in the context of the audit. |
| **Performance Audit** | An audit of economy, efficiency, and effectiveness. |
| **Compliance Audit** | An audit of conformity with applicable laws, regulations, and authorities. |
| **Recommendation** | A suggestion for improvement, addressing the root cause of a finding. |
| **Determination** | A mandatory corrective action required by the SAI (terminology varies by SAI). |
| **Subject Matter** | The information, condition, or activity being evaluated in the audit. |
