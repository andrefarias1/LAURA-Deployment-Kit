# Audit Methodology Reference — Generic Frameworks & Techniques
## LAURA Core Knowledge Base

> **Purpose:** This document provides LAURA with generic, SAI-agnostic knowledge of audit methodology — risk assessment, evidence collection techniques, analysis methods, and quality assurance. It complements the ISSAI Core Reference and the Findings & Reporting Framework. SAIs should supplement this with their own methodology manuals.

---

## 1. The Audit Lifecycle

Every public sector audit follows a lifecycle with four macro-phases:

```
PLANNING → EXECUTION → REPORTING → MONITORING
```

LAURA covers the first three phases in detail (P0–P7 for Planning, E0–E8 for Execution & Reporting). Monitoring is post-report and typically outside LAURA's operational scope, but the audit plan should anticipate it.

### 1.1 Planning Phase — Overview
Planning establishes the foundation for the entire audit. A well-planned audit:
- Defines clear objectives and scope
- Identifies risks and key audit questions
- Establishes appropriate criteria
- Designs data collection strategies
- Estimates resources, timelines, and costs

Key deliverables (generic):
- Audit Terms / Audit Canvas (strategic overview)
- Subject Matter Overview (understanding the audit object)
- Risk Assessment / Risk Matrix
- Audit Design Matrix (detailed planning of procedures per question)
- Data collection instruments (interview guides, questionnaires, checklists)
- Audit Plan (consolidated planning document)
- Quality review of the planning package

### 1.2 Execution Phase — Overview
Execution is the fieldwork phase where auditors:
- Collect evidence using planned instruments and techniques
- Analyse evidence against established criteria
- Develop audit findings
- Structure findings into a Findings Matrix
- (If applicable) Develop an Accountability Matrix for irregularities
- Draft the audit report

### 1.3 Reporting Phase — Overview
Reporting communicates the audit results:
- Draft the report following the SAI's structure and style guidelines
- Verify that recommendations/determinations comply with the SAI's standards
- Submit preliminary findings for auditee comment (adversarial/contradictory process)
- Incorporate auditee comments
- Conduct editorial and quality review
- Export/finalise the report for institutional approval

## 2. Risk Assessment Frameworks

### 2.1 The Audit Risk Model
The traditional audit risk model:

```
Audit Risk = Inherent Risk × Control Risk × Detection Risk
```

- **Inherent Risk (IR):** The susceptibility of the subject matter to material misstatement or non-compliance, before considering internal controls. Factors include: complexity, volume, nature of transactions, political sensitivity, prior findings.
- **Control Risk (CR):** The risk that the entity's internal controls will fail to prevent or detect material problems. Factors include: control design, implementation effectiveness, monitoring.
- **Detection Risk (DR):** The risk that the auditor's procedures will not identify material problems. This is the risk the auditor manages through the design of audit procedures.

### 2.2 Risk Assessment in Performance Audits (ECA Model)
The European Court of Auditors (ECA) risk assessment approach structures each risk as:

**Cause → Event → Consequence**

For each identified risk:
1. **Causes:** What vulnerabilities or conditions could trigger the risk?
2. **Event:** What undesired situation could occur?
3. **Consequences:** What impact would this have on the audit object's objectives?
4. **Impact assessment:** High / Medium / Low
5. **Probability assessment:** High / Medium / Low
6. **Risk level:** Derived from Impact × Probability (e.g., Trivial / Moderate / Intolerable)
7. **Risk response:** Accept, mitigate, or address through audit procedures

### 2.3 COSO Enterprise Risk Management (2017)
The COSO ERM framework provides a comprehensive approach for evaluating an auditee's risk management. Its five components are:

1. **Governance and Culture** — Board oversight, operating structures, core values, human capital
2. **Strategy and Objective-Setting** — Business context, risk appetite, strategy alignment
3. **Performance** — Risk identification, risk assessment, risk prioritisation, risk responses, portfolio view
4. **Review and Revision** — Monitoring changes, review of risk and performance, improvement
5. **Information, Communication, and Reporting** — Leveraging information systems, communicating risk information, reporting on risk, culture, and performance

Auditors use COSO ERM to:
- Evaluate the maturity of the auditee's risk management
- Identify control gaps
- Assess the design and operating effectiveness of internal controls
- Provide recommendations for risk management improvement

### 2.4 Risk Syntax (Standardised Risk Description)
For consistent risk documentation, LAURA uses a standardised syntax:

> **Due to** [CAUSE — source of vulnerability], **there is a risk that** [EVENT — including the criteria at risk], **which could lead to** [EFFECTS — negative consequences], **and impact** [IMPACTS — on the entity's objectives regarding cost, timeliness, quality, or scope].

Each risk receives a sequential code (e.g., RIS-01, RIS-02, ..., RIS-XX).

## 3. Data Collection Techniques

### 3.1 Document / Desk Review
- Systematic review of relevant documents: legislation, policies, procedures, contracts, reports, financial records, prior audit reports.
- Creates a documentary evidence base.
- Should be structured: list documents, summarise key content, note relevance to audit questions.

### 3.2 Interviews
**Types:** Structured (predetermined questions), semi-structured (guide with flexibility), unstructured (open-ended).

**Best practices:**
- Prepare an interview guide aligned with audit questions.
- Identify appropriate interviewees (decision-makers, technical staff, beneficiaries).
- Record the interview (with consent) and produce a written summary.
- Share the summary with the interviewee for validation.
- Minimum two auditors present: one to lead, one to take notes.

**Interview guide structure:**
1. Opening: context, objectives, confidentiality
2. Core questions: organised by theme/audit question
3. Follow-up: emerging topics, clarifications
4. Closing: next steps, opportunity for interviewee to add points

### 3.3 Surveys / Questionnaires
- Used when information is needed from many respondents.
- Can be quantitative (closed-ended) or qualitative (open-ended).
- Enable statistical treatment and graphical representation.
- Must be piloted before full deployment.

**Design principles:**
- Clear, unambiguous questions
- Logical flow (general → specific)
- Appropriate response options (avoid double-barrelled questions)
- Include instructions for respondents
- Consider response rate strategies

### 3.4 Focus Groups
- Group discussion technique (6–8 participants) to capture diverse perspectives.
- Useful for understanding complex behaviours, motivations, and perceptions.
- Requires: facilitator + note-taker, discussion guide (10–12 questions for ≤2 hours).
- Conduct 4–6 groups per category to reach saturation.
- Analyse results using content analysis techniques.
- Cannot generalise statistically; provides qualitative depth.

### 3.5 Direct Observation / Site Visits
- First-hand examination of processes, conditions, or physical assets.
- Produces physical evidence.
- Should be planned, structured (observation checklist), and documented (photos, notes).

### 3.6 Data Analytics
- Use of quantitative or computational methods to analyse large datasets.
- Includes: statistical analysis, trend analysis, regression, benchmarking, geospatial analysis.
- Increasing importance in modern audit practice.
- Requires understanding of data quality, integrity, and limitations.

### 3.7 Benchmarking / Comparative Analysis
- Comparing the audit object's performance against peers, best practices, or international standards.
- Types: internal benchmarking, external benchmarking, international benchmarking.
- Useful for establishing criteria when normative criteria are absent.

### 3.8 Case Studies
- In-depth examination of specific instances to illustrate broader patterns.
- Useful in performance audit for understanding "how" and "why" questions.
- Selection should be purposive and justified.

### 3.9 Expert Panels / Advisory Groups
- Consultation with subject-matter experts to validate criteria, methods, or findings.
- Useful when the audit topic is technically complex.
- Document expert qualifications, contributions, and any limitations.

## 4. Evidence Standards and Management

### 4.1 Evidence Attributes
All audit evidence must be:
- **Relevant:** Logically connected to the audit objective, question, and criteria.
- **Sufficient:** Enough in quantity and quality to support a reasonable conclusion.
- **Valid:** Factually correct, well-sourced, and verifiable by others.
- **Reliable:** Obtained from credible sources using sound methods.

### 4.2 Types of Evidence
| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Physical | Obtained through observation | Site visit reports, photographs, inspection records |
| Documentary | Obtained from written records | Official documents, contracts, financial statements, emails |
| Testimonial | Obtained from people | Interview summaries, survey results, expert opinions |
| Analytical | Derived from analysis | Statistical outputs, calculations, comparisons, trend analyses |

### 4.3 Evidence Documentation
Each piece of evidence should be catalogued with:
- Unique identifier (e.g., EV-001, EV-002)
- Description
- Date obtained
- Source
- Collection method / technique
- Linkage to audit question and finding
- Quality assessment (sufficient? relevant? reliable?)

### 4.4 Evidence Quality Issues
When evidence is insufficient, the auditor should:
- Seek additional evidence from alternative sources
- Apply complementary techniques (triangulation)
- Document the limitation and its impact on conclusions
- Adjust the assurance level if necessary

## 5. Sampling

### 5.1 When to Sample
When the population is too large or dispersed to examine entirely, sampling provides a practical and defensible approach.

### 5.2 Sampling Methods
- **Statistical (probability) sampling:** Random, systematic, stratified. Allows generalisation to the population with quantified confidence levels.
- **Non-statistical (judgemental) sampling:** Selected based on auditor judgement, risk, or specific criteria. Does not allow statistical generalisation but may be appropriate for qualitative assessments.

### 5.3 Key Sampling Decisions
1. Define the population clearly
2. Determine the sampling unit
3. Choose the sampling method
4. Calculate sample size (considering confidence level, expected error rate, tolerable error)
5. Select and examine the sample
6. Evaluate results and project to the population (for statistical samples)
7. Document the sampling methodology and results

## 6. Analysis Techniques

### 6.1 Problem Analysis
- **Problem Tree:** Identifies a core problem, its causes (roots), and effects (branches).
- **Root Cause Analysis:** Systematic identification of underlying causes using techniques like "5 Whys", Ishikawa/fishbone diagrams.
- **Logic Model / Theory of Change:** Maps inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → impact.

### 6.2 Stakeholder Analysis
Identify all relevant actors and their:
- Interests in the audit object
- Influence over the audit object
- Potential impact on the audit
- Information needs regarding audit results

### 6.3 SWOT Analysis
Assessment of the audit object's:
- **Strengths** (internal positive factors)
- **Weaknesses** (internal negative factors)
- **Opportunities** (external positive factors)
- **Threats** (external negative factors)

### 6.4 RACI/RECI Matrix
Maps roles and responsibilities:
- **R** — Responsible (does the work)
- **A/E** — Accountable/Executor (ultimately answerable)
- **C** — Consulted (provides input)
- **I** — Informed (kept in the loop)

### 6.5 Process Mapping
Visual representation of how a process works (flowcharts, swimlane diagrams). Identifies:
- Steps, decision points, handoffs
- Bottlenecks, redundancies, control gaps
- Opportunities for improvement

### 6.6 Content Analysis
Systematic analysis of textual data (interview transcripts, open-ended survey responses, documents) to identify patterns, themes, and categories.

## 7. Quality Assurance Framework

### 7.1 Supervision
- Ongoing oversight by the audit supervisor throughout all phases.
- Ensures work complies with standards and stays on track.
- Includes regular team meetings, review of progress, guidance on issues.

### 7.2 Review of Work Papers
- All work papers should be reviewed by someone other than the preparer.
- Review checks for: completeness, accuracy, logical flow, evidence support, compliance with standards.

### 7.3 Quality Checklist (Generic)
A quality review should cover:
- [ ] Audit objective is clear, relevant, and achievable
- [ ] Scope is well-defined and appropriate
- [ ] Criteria are relevant, complete, and from authoritative sources
- [ ] Audit questions are answerable and cover the objective
- [ ] Audit Design Matrix is complete and internally consistent
- [ ] Data collection instruments are appropriate and tested
- [ ] Evidence is sufficient, relevant, and reliable for each finding
- [ ] Findings are structured (Condition, Criteria, Cause, Effect)
- [ ] Conclusions are supported by findings
- [ ] Recommendations address root causes and are feasible
- [ ] Auditee comments have been considered (adversarial principle)
- [ ] Report follows the SAI's format and style standards
- [ ] Report language is objective, impersonal, and clear
- [ ] All references, annexes, and appendices are correct

### 7.4 Independence and Ethics
Before and during each audit, verify:
- No conflicts of interest among team members
- Compliance with the SAI's code of ethics
- Organisational, functional, and financial independence maintained

## 8. The Audit Canvas — Generic Template

The Audit Canvas provides a strategic overview of the audit at the beginning of planning. Generic components:

| # | Component | Description |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | **Subject Matter / Audit Object** | What is being audited? (delimited physically, spatially, temporally) |
| 2 | **Criteria** | What standards will be used to evaluate the object? |
| 3 | **Evidence Strategy** | What types of evidence will be sought? |
| 4 | **Audit Problem** | What situation justifies this audit? |
| 5 | **Stakeholders** | Who are the interested parties and what are their interests? |
| 6 | **Assurance Level** | Reasonable or limited assurance? |
| 7 | **Audit Objective** | What does the audit aim to achieve? (SMART format) |
| 8 | **Out of Scope** | What will NOT be covered? |
| 9 | **Scope** | Entities, departments, time periods, processes, systems |
| 10 | **Risks** | Key risks to the audit engagement |
| 11 | **Deliverables** | Expected products for each phase |
| 12 | **Timeline** | Key dates and milestones |
| 13 | **Expected Benefits** | Financial and non-financial benefits of the audit |
